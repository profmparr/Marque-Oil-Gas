The Isle of Man Gas Opportunity

Local Gas. Lower Emissions. Long-Term Public Value.

Situated within the Isle of Man’s territorial waters lies a potentially significant gas discovery. The gas development could deliver billions of pounds in tax revenue to the Isle of Man.

The gas field sits directly beneath the proposed Mooir Vannin wind farm, however, the gas field cannot be developed if the wind farm is in place. It may be possible to develop the wind farm once the gas field has been developed. There is thus economic competition for the same sea-bed area, with the choice of:

  1. Gas only.
  2. Wind only.
  3. Gas + wind – but NOT wind + gas.

The Case For Manx Gas

Strategic advantages of developing local Manx gas resources.

Energy Security

Local gas could provide energy security to shield the Island from global supply shortages.

Price Security

Could provide capped gas prices to IoM customers to prevent energy price spikes feeding through to household bills.

Tax Revenue

Will provide billions of pounds of tax revenue to the island.

Net Zero Compliance

Will reduce CO2 emissions as local gas has lower emissions than imported gas, and could form part of a UK Carbon Capture and Storage system to provide emissions-free electricity to the Island.

Tiny Visual Environmental Impact

Sea-bed well-heads connecting to a 30m tall unmanned installation approximately 10 miles off the coast.

No CO2 Emissions at Source

Compressor power supplied from shore.

The Development Vision

Step-by-step progression of the local gas development and carbon capture integration.

1

Barrow Processing

Gas piped to Barrow for processing at existing facility (dashed red line and red triangle).

2

Domestic Supply

10% of gas piped back to the IoM for domestic supplies through existing pipeline network and Irish interconnector spur (solid red line).

3

Connah’s Quay Generation

90% of gas piped to Connah’s Quay for electricity generation and CO2 emissions capture as part of the CCS scheme.

4

Carbon Storage

Captured CO2 emissions compressed and piped (purple line) to the Hamilton & Lennox Carbon Store for underground storage in depleted gas production reservoir.

5

Clean Interconnector

Emissions-free electricity supplies the Isle of Man through a new interconnector (black dashed line).

The Economic Comparison in Brief

Key economic tests for Isle of Man residents, Government finances and local impacts

Economic test Wind Power and Mooir Vannin Gas Development
Price protection NO — no protection from gas/electricity price spikes. YES — lower prices and capped surges for residents.
Project support NO — no UK CfDs/curtailment payments; may need guarantees. YES — no Government price guarantees required.
Exchequer benefit UNLIKELY — the stated £2bn benefit is not secure. YES — benefits expected to exceed wind farm upside.
Resident bills RISK — potential move from 29p/kWh to ~45p/kWh (+50%). YES — keeps electricity prices low, free of renewable tariffs.
Wider Local economy BAD — visual impact concern; may negatively impact tourism and fishing. NEUTRAL — installations not expected to damage tourism or significantly affect fishing.

The Bottom Line: A Simple Choice

  • Option A: A wind farm that raises bills and brings little benefit, or
  • Option B: A gas development that brings in higher tax revenues and keeps prices low.
